LIKELY QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS

Problem framing

Q. Why forecast deaths and not events?
Deaths are the measure of harm that planners act on, and the series is comparable across the four conflicts. Events drive media volume, not lethality, as our index shows.
Q. Why only these four conflicts?
They are the competition scope. The pipeline is generic: any UCDP conflict can be added by extending the case list in config.py.

Data and cleaning

Q. Did you change the source data?
No. Raw CSVs are read-only. Repairs go into new columns (e.g. deaths_low_c) and every change is in the 36-check data-quality log.
Q. How did you handle multi-month events?
731 records span more than one month, including front-wide aggregates. We spread their deaths across months by day count, otherwise the start month shows a false spike.
Q. What about out-of-order low/best/high?
692 rows. We keep best, because it reconciles exactly with the fatality components, and widen the band to min/max. Raw values are kept.
Q. Why merge FATA into KP?
Pakistan merged them in 2018. Without harmonising, FATA would show a fake collapse and KP a fake surge exactly at the boundary change.
Q. 2026 data are provisional; is the test fair?
It is the hardest, most realistic test: operational users always work on provisional data. Revisions usually go upward, which makes our holdout error conservative.

Models and validation

Q. Why not a deep-learning model (LSTM)?
Four series of 153 months is far too little data. Our ablation shows even a 40-feature LightGBM over-fits. Simpler, regularised models generalise better and are explainable.
Q. Why does LightGBM predict the change, not the level?
Trees cannot predict above the highest value seen in training. A level model trained before 2022 can never forecast the invasion. The change target removes that ceiling: back-test RMSLE 1.23 (level) vs 0.90 (change, compact).
Q. How do you know you didn't tune on 2026?
All choices (features, ensemble members, interval bands, classifier features) were made on 2022–25 folds. 2026 was scored once. On Track B SARIMA beat the ensemble in 2026, and we report that rather than switch.
Q. Why is RMSLE the selection metric?
The series differ by three orders of magnitude. RMSE would be dominated by Russia–Ukraine. RMSLE scores relative error fairly. MAE, RMSE, MAPE, sMAPE and MASE are all reported too.
Q. Your Af–Pak improvement is only 4%. Why?
Af–Pak is a steady creep with little month-to-month signal, so 'no change' is already a strong baseline. The ensemble still wins, and the gain is large where it matters (RU–UA, Israel–Hamas, US–Iran).
Q. Why are the 95% intervals so wide?
The back-test includes the 2022 invasion. A calibrated interval must keep room for outbreaks. The 80% band, which covered 83% of 2026, is the planning band.
Q. The model missed the US–Iran war. Isn't that a failure?
Yes, and we say so. No death-count model can foresee a decision to go to war. That is why warning also tracks spread and hotspots, and why the scorecard has a volatility rule.
Q. How good is the early warning, really?
AUC 0.79 on 2026 (rule of thumb 0.73); top-10 precision 14% vs base rate 4.1%. Escalations are rare (4%), so precision looks low in absolute terms, but it is 3.5× random: it focuses scarce ISR.

Military and India

Q. Is the Russia:Ukraine 10:1 ratio real?
No; it is recorded. Ukrainian OSINT and memorial databases record Russian deaths more fully. We show it as an upper bound of reporting and caveat it on the slide.
Q. How do you link the Af–Pak war to the LoC?
The data show Pakistan fighting on its western front. The LoC link is domain reasoning, labelled DOMAIN, and we recommend it as an indicator to watch, not a prediction.
Q. Where does '9 million Indians in the Gulf' come from?
It is public MEA-order-of-magnitude knowledge, not from the competition files, and is labelled DOMAIN.
Q. What would you do with classified data?
Fuse it as extra features (e.g. movement indicators). The pipeline already accepts new features; the walk-forward protocol would test their value.

Dashboard and reproducibility

Q. Can it run offline at an HQ?
Yes. Streamlit on a laptop, no internet calls, bundled map outline. One command rebuilds everything in about 2.5 minutes.
Q. Can it be updated monthly?
Drop in the new UCDP month and rerun. The 'live re-train' page refits LightGBM-Δ in about 2 seconds.
Q. How was AI used?
As a coding assistant, which the rules allow. Every number is produced by code in the repository and every result can be reproduced and checked.
